Helping communities prepare for a changing climate.

HESO supports communities to understand climate-related risks, protect natural resources, strengthen livelihoods, and build practical solutions that improve resilience over time.

Get involved ↗
01 Why Resilience Matters

Climate change affects the things communities depend on.

Changes in rainfall, temperature, water availability, extreme weather, and environmental conditions can affect health, agriculture, livelihoods, and access to natural resources.

Building resilience means helping communities understand these risks and strengthen their ability to prepare, respond, adapt, and recover.

03 · Community-led

Resilience starts with people.

Climate resilience is not only about technology or infrastructure. It also depends on knowledge, cooperation, local leadership, and the ability of communities to make informed decisions.

HESO works with communities and local stakeholders to identify challenges, understand priorities, and develop responses that reflect local realities.

04 · Nature & Climate

Healthy ecosystems make communities stronger.

Forests, wetlands, soils, rivers, and other natural systems provide resources and services that communities rely on. Protecting them is part of building long-term resilience.

Forests Supporting conservation and responsible management of natural vegetation.
Soils Promoting practices that maintain productive and healthy soils.
Water Sources Encouraging protection of rivers, springs, wetlands, and other water resources.
Biodiversity Recognizing the role of healthy ecosystems in resilient communities.
05 · Agriculture

Helping farmers adapt while protecting the land.

Agriculture depends heavily on climate and natural resources. HESO promotes approaches that can help farmers manage changing conditions while maintaining productive and sustainable land.

06 · Climate & Health

Environmental change can become a health challenge.

Climate and environmental conditions can influence water availability, food security, living conditions, and other factors that affect community well-being.

HESO connects climate resilience with public health, environmental protection, WASH, agriculture, and community education.
